Open methodology

Three steps from ball to shortlist.

Rules are evaluated before each delivery, so the ball being scored cannot create its own pressure tag. The same approach is applied separately to batting and bowling.

01

Tag the moment

Chases: required rate at least 9, or 5 or fewer wickets left after 10 overs, or the final five overs. First innings: final five overs with 5 or fewer wickets left.

02

Compare with self

Batters use strike rate and dismissal rate. Bowlers use economy and bowler-credited wicket rate. A player's pressure numbers are measured against their own non-pressure baseline.

03

Reward repeat evidence

Minimum pressure and ordinary ball counts plus 8 distinct pressure matches screen thin samples. A continuous shrinkage factor pulls lower-exposure advantages toward zero.

Batter score

(Pressure SR − Usual SR) − 0.60 × (Pressure dismissal rate − Usual dismissal rate)
Then multiply the raw advantage by pressure balls / (pressure balls + 60).

SR = runs per 100 balls faced

Bowler score

(Usual economy − Pressure economy) × 100 / 6 + 0.60 × (Pressure wicket rate − Usual wicket rate)
Then apply the same exposure shrinkage.

Wicket rates = bowler wickets per 100 legal balls
Trust the evidence

Useful signal. Honest limits.

The interface keeps the ranking decision and its caveats together, so judges and cricket analysts can understand what the score does and does not say.

Fair comparison checks

  • Each player is compared with their own ordinary deliveries rather than raw career totals.
  • Minimum exposure: batters need 60 pressure balls and 200 ordinary balls; bowlers need 60 pressure legal balls and 180 ordinary legal balls.
  • Each eligible player must appear in at least 8 distinct pressure matches.
  • Wides do not count as balls faced; wides and no-ball penalties remain in bowler runs. Super overs are excluded.
  • Revised targets and innings allocations are read from the supplied match data when available.

Interpretation limits

This is a transparent heuristic, not a win-probability or causal model. It does not fully adjust for opposition quality, venue, batter position, bowling role, season changes, or all differences between IPL and T20I competition.

Close scores should be read as a cluster. The wicket-risk weight (0.60), pressure cutoffs, and sample windows affect the order. Rankings are based on the supplied data and should not be presented as a guarantee of future performance.

For the 2027 auction: use the pressure list as the first filter. A final recommendation also depends on RR's confirmed roster, eligibility, availability, role overlap, overseas slots, fitness, and auction price.
